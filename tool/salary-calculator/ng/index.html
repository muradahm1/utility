Understanding Nigerian PAYE Tax, Pension, CRA, and Take-Home Pay

In Nigeria, employment income tax is deducted at source under the Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) system governed by the Personal Income Tax Act (PITA) Cap P8 LFN 2004, amended by recent Finance Acts. Administered by State Internal Revenue Services (such as LIRS in Lagos, FCT-IRS in Abuja, and others), PAYE withholding is calculated after applying statutory reliefs including the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) and pension contributions.

Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) Explained

Under PITA Section 33, every employee in Nigeria is entitled to a Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA). The CRA is calculated as the higher of ₦200,000 per annum (₦16,666.67 per month) or 1% of Gross Income, plus an additional 20% of Gross Income. The CRA significantly reduces taxable income before progressive tax brackets are applied, protecting lower and middle-income earners from excessive taxation.

Mandatory Pension Contributions (Pension Reform Act 2014)

The Pension Reform Act (PRA) 2014 mandates that formal sector employers with 3 or more employees contribute to a contributory pension scheme. The employee contributes a minimum of 8% of monthly pensionable emoluments (defined as Basic Salary, Housing Allowance, and Transport Allowance), while the employer contributes at least 10%. Crucially, employee pension contributions are completely tax-exempt, deducted directly from gross income before calculating chargeable pay.

PITA Sixth Schedule Tax Brackets & Rates

After deducting CRA, pension contributions, and NHF from gross income, the remaining Chargeable Income is taxed across six progressive monthly brackets: the first ₦25,000 (₦300k/yr) at 7%; the next ₦25,000 (₦300k/yr) at 11%; the next ₦41,667 (₦500k/yr) at 15%; the next ₦41,667 (₦500k/yr) at 19%; the next ₦133,333 (₦1.6M/yr) at 21%; and any amount above ₦266,667 per month (₦3.2M/yr) at the top rate of 24%.

Minimum Wage Exemption & Minimum Tax Rule

Under the Finance Act, employees earning the National Minimum Wage (₦70,000 per month) or less are fully exempt from personal income tax. For employees earning above the minimum wage, if the computed PAYE tax is less than 1% of gross income, a statutory Minimum Tax equal to 1% of gross income applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is PAYE tax calculated in Nigeria in 2026?

PAYE in Nigeria is calculated by subtracting Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA), 8% employee pension, and 2.5% NHF from Gross Emoluments to arrive at Chargeable Income. Progressive tax rates ranging from 7% to 24% are then applied across brackets.

What is the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) in Nigeria?

CRA is a statutory tax relief under PITA Section 33 equal to the higher of ₦200,000 per year (₦16,666.67/mo) or 1% of Gross Income, PLUS 20% of Gross Income.

Are workers on minimum wage exempt from tax in Nigeria?

Yes. Under the Finance Act, individuals earning the national minimum wage (₦70,000 per month) or less are completely exempt from personal income tax and minimum tax provisions.

How is pension calculated under PRA 2014?

The employee contributes a minimum of 8% of monthly pensionable emoluments (Basic Salary + Housing Allowance + Transport Allowance), and the employer contributes a minimum of 10%. Employee pension contributions are 100% tax-free.

What is the National Housing Fund (NHF) in Nigeria?

The NHF requires employees earning above minimum wage to contribute 2.5% of their basic monthly salary to the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria to qualify for low-interest residential mortgage loans.
