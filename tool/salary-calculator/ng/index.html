Complete Guide to Nigerian PAYE, Pension, and Tax Act 2025 Reform

In Nigeria, employee payroll is governed by federal legislation administered by State Internal Revenue Services (such as LIRS in Lagos) and the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS). The landmark Nigeria Tax Act 2025 overhauled personal taxation, abolishing the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) in favor of a ₦800,000 tax-free personal band and revised progressive brackets, while maintaining mandatory retirement savings under the Pension Reform Act (PRA) 2014.

Nigeria Tax Act 2025 vs. PITA CRA Reform

Prior to the 2025 reform, Nigerian employees deducted a Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) equal to the higher of ₦200,000 or 1% of gross, plus 20% of gross income. Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective January 1, 2026), the CRA is eliminated. In its place, the tax schedule introduces a 0% tax-exempt band on the first ₦800,000 of annual taxable earnings (₦66,666.67/month), protecting low earners from tax liability without complex relief calculations.

Progressive Tax Rates Under Nigeria Tax Act 2025

Annual taxable income above ₦800,000 is taxed in progressive brackets: ₦800,001 to ₦3,000,000 at 15%; ₦3,000,001 to ₦12,000,000 at 18%; ₦12,000,001 to ₦25,000,000 at 21%; ₦25,000,001 to ₦50,000,000 at 23%; and taxable income exceeding ₦50,000,000 at 25%. Historical calculations under the PITA 6-bracket schedule (7% to 24%) remain fully accessible for past tax periods.

Rent Relief Deduction

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 introduced an allowable deduction for personal rent paid. Employees who submit valid tenancy documentation can deduct actual rent paid, capped at the lower of ₦500,000 per annum (₦41,666.67 per month) or 20% of their actual annual rent, directly reducing chargeable income.

Mandatory Pension Reform Act (PRA) 2014

Under the Pension Reform Act 2014, employers and employees must contribute to a Retirement Savings Account (RSA). The employee contributes a mandatory minimum of 8%, while the employer contributes at least 10%. Contributions are calculated on pensionable emoluments, defined as the sum of Basic Salary, Housing Allowance, and Transport Allowance.

Nigeria Salary & PAYE Tax Calculator Formula

Chargeable Income = Gross Salary − Employee Pension (8%) − NHF (2.5% Basic) − Rent Relief (or CRA under PITA); Net Pay = Gross Salary − PAYE Tax − Pension − NHF − Deductions

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 change PAYE calculations?

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 abolished the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA). In its place, the first ₦800,000 per year (₦66,666.67/month) is completely tax-exempt at 0%. Income above ₦800k is taxed in progressive brackets of 15%, 18%, 21%, 23%, and 25%.

What is Rent Relief under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025?

Rent Relief allows employees to deduct rent paid for accommodation from their gross income before tax, up to the lesser of ₦500,000 per year (₦41,666.67/month) or 20% of their actual annual rent.

Can I still calculate tax under the historical PITA CRA framework?

Yes. GetCalcu allows you to toggle to "PITA 2004 & Finance Acts" from the Tax Law Framework dropdown to calculate past payroll periods with the Consolidated Relief Allowance and 7%–24% brackets.

How is pension calculated in Nigeria?

Under the Pension Reform Act 2014, the employee contributes a minimum of 8% and the employer contributes 10% calculated on pensionable emoluments: Basic Salary + Housing Allowance + Transport Allowance.

What is the National Housing Fund (NHF) deduction?

The NHF Act stipulates a 2.5% deduction from an employee's monthly basic salary, remitted to the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria.
