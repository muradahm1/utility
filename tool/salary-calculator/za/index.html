Understanding South African PAYE Tax, UIF, Rebates, and Take-Home Pay

In South Africa, individual employment earnings are subject to Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) income tax withholding administered by the South African Revenue Service (SARS) under the Income Tax Act No. 58 of 1962. Net take-home salary is calculated by deducting allowable retirement contributions from gross income, applying progressive tax tables, and offsetting the resulting tax liability with statutory age-based rebates and medical aid tax credits.

SARS Progressive Tax Brackets (2026/2027 Tax Year)

For the 2026/2027 tax assessment year (1 March 2026 – 28 February 2027), South Africa operates 7 progressive brackets: R0 to R245,100 at 18%; R245,101 to R383,100 at 26%; R383,101 to R530,200 at 31%; R530,201 to R695,800 at 36%; R695,801 to R887,000 at 39%; R887,001 to R1,878,600 at 41%; and earnings exceeding R1,878,600 at 45%.

Section 6 Statutory Tax Rebates & Thresholds

For the 2027 tax year, the Primary Tax Rebate is R17,820 per annum (R1,485.00 per month). Taxpayers aged 65 to 74 receive an additional Secondary Rebate of R9,765 per annum (total rebate R27,585). Taxpayers aged 75 and older receive an additional Tertiary Rebate of R3,249 per annum (total rebate R30,834). Because of these rebates, taxpayers under 65 earning under the tax threshold of R99,000 per year (R8,250 per month) pay zero income tax.

Section 11F Retirement Deductions (Pension, Provident & RA)

Contributions made to pension funds, provident funds, and retirement annuity (RA) funds are tax-deductible under Section 11F of the Income Tax Act. The allowable deduction is capped at the lesser of actual contributions, 27.5% of remuneration, or R350,000 per annum (R29,166.67 per month).

Section 6A Medical Scheme Fees Tax Credit (MTC)

Under Section 6A for the 2027 tax year, the monthly non-refundable tax credit is R376 for the primary member, R376 for the first dependent, and R254 for each additional dependent, directly offsetting PAYE tax payable.

Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) Contributions

Both employee and employer contribute 1% of gross remuneration to the UIF each month, subject to the statutory monthly earnings ceiling of R17,712 per month (maximum employee deduction of R177.12 per month).

Frequently Asked Questions

How is PAYE tax calculated in South Africa for the 2026/2027 tax year?

PAYE is calculated on annual taxable remuneration (gross income minus Section 11F retirement deductions) using the 7 progressive SARS tax brackets (18% to 45%). The gross tax is reduced by Section 6 statutory rebates (Primary R17,820) and Section 6A medical aid tax credits (R376/mo for main member and first dependent).

What is the maximum UIF deduction in South Africa?

The employee UIF deduction is 1% of gross remuneration, capped at the statutory ceiling of R17,712 per month, resulting in a maximum monthly employee deduction of R177.12.

How much of my pension or RA contribution is tax deductible?

Under Section 11F of the Income Tax Act, contributions to pension, provident, and retirement annuity funds are deductible up to 27.5% of remuneration, subject to an overall statutory maximum of R350,000 per year (R29,166.67 per month).

How do medical aid tax credits (Section 6A) work in 2026/2027?

Medical Scheme Fees Tax Credits are fixed non-refundable monthly credits that reduce your PAYE tax: R376 for the main member, R376 for the first dependent, and R254 for each additional dependent.
