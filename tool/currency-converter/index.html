How Foreign Exchange & Currency Conversion Works

Currency conversion allows travelers, international businesses, and investors to exchange values between different world currencies based on prevailing foreign exchange (forex) market rates.

Mid-Market Rates vs. Retail Bank Rates

The mid-market rate (or interbank rate) is the midpoint between global buy and sell prices. Consumer banks and airport kiosks usually add a 1.5% to 4% margin or commission on top of this rate.

How to Avoid Excessive Foreign Transaction Fees

When spending abroad or shopping on international websites, always choose to be billed in the local currency of the seller rather than using dynamic currency conversion (DCC), which often charges high markups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mid-market exchange rate?

The mid-market exchange rate is the real midpoint between the supply and demand for a currency in global markets without bank markups.

Why do banks give a different rate than online calculators?

Commercial banks and exchange booths add a retail markup (spread) to make a profit. Calculators typically display the mid-market reference rate.

What is Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC)?

DCC occurs when an overseas ATM or card terminal asks if you want to be charged in your home currency. It usually carries poor exchange rates (3–7% markup). It is usually cheaper to choose the local currency.
