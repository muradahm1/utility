15-Year vs. 30-Year Mortgage: The Comprehensive Decision Guide

Deciding between a 15-year and 30-year mortgage is a foundational strategic choice between guaranteed debt elimination and monthly cash flow flexibility. Comparing amortization trajectories reveals where every dollar goes.

When a 15-Year Loan Makes Sense vs. When a 30-Year Makes Sense

A 15-year mortgage makes sense when you have high, stable income and prioritize debt freedom. You secure a lower interest rate (typically 0.50% to 1.00% below 30-year rates), build equity more than four times faster in the early years, and eliminate mortgage payments before college tuition or retirement arrive.

A 30-year mortgage makes sense when you need budget breathing room or want to maximize long-term wealth through investing. The lower mandatory payment protects against unexpected job disruptions or medical costs. Meanwhile, investing the $598.96 monthly savings into diversified index funds can yield a larger net worth over 30 years than paying down low-interest debt early.

Worked Example: Month-by-Month Interest Comparison ($320,000 Loan)

Consider a $320,000 mortgage comparing a 15-year note at 5.85% against a 30-year note at 6.75%:

Month 1 Breakdown: On the 15-year loan ($2,674.48 P&I), Month 1 interest is $1,560.00 and $1,114.48 goes directly toward principal. On the 30-year loan ($2,075.51 P&I), Month 1 interest is $1,800.00 and only $275.51 goes toward principal. In month one alone, the 15-year loan saves $240.00 in interest and pays off four times as much debt.

Year 1 Milestone (Month 12): After 12 payments, the 15-year loan balance drops to $306,261.79 ($13,738.21 principal paid; $18,355.55 interest). The 30-year loan balance remains at $316,589.61 ($3,410.39 principal paid; $21,495.73 interest). In just one year, the 15-year homeowner builds $10,327.82 more home equity and saves $3,140.18 in interest.

Lifetime Summary: Over the full term, the 15-year loan incurs $161,406.06 in total interest versus $427,185.01 on the 30-year loan, delivering guaranteed lifetime interest savings of $265,778.94.

Guaranteed Return vs. Market Opportunity Cost

Paying off a 15-year mortgage at 5.85% provides a guaranteed, risk-free 5.85% after-tax return on your principal. Conversely, selecting a 30-year loan and systematically investing the $598.96 monthly savings into low-cost index funds (which historically have returned 8% to 10% annualized) offers higher expected returns, subject to market volatility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 15-year mortgage always better than a 30-year mortgage?

No. While a 15-year mortgage saves massive interest, the higher mandatory payment restricts cash flow. If investing the monthly difference in tax-advantaged retirement accounts (401k/IRA), a 30-year mortgage can actually yield a higher overall net worth.

How much do I save with a 15-year mortgage on a $320,000 loan?

At baseline rates of 5.85% for 15 years and 6.75% for 30 years, a $320,000 loan saves $265,778.94 in total interest ($161,406.06 vs. $427,185.01). The 15-year monthly principal and interest payment is $2,674.48, compared to $2,075.51 on the 30-year loan (a difference of $598.96 per month).

Can I pay off a 30-year mortgage in 15 years?

Yes! Standard mortgages in the US have no prepayment penalties. You can take a 30-year loan for safety and make extra principal payments each month to retire the loan in 15 years.

How much lower is the interest rate on a 15-year mortgage?

Typically, 15-year fixed mortgage rates are 0.50% to 1.00% lower than 30-year fixed rates because lenders take on less duration risk.

What happens to my payment when the 15-year loan is paid off?

Your principal and interest payment drops to $0. You only pay property taxes, hazard insurance, and maintenance costs going forward.
