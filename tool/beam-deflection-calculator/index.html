Understanding Beam Deflection in Structural Engineering

Beam deflection is the degree to which a beam bends under load. Excessive deflection can damage finishes and affect structural integrity.

The Deflection Formula

Deflection depends on load, span length, material stiffness (E), and cross-section shape (I). The formulas differ for simply supported and cantilever beams.

Allowable Deflection Limits

Building codes typically limit deflection to L/360 for live loads and L/240 for dead loads. For a 10 ft beam, that is about 0.33 inches for live load.

Increasing Stiffness

To reduce deflection, increase the moment of inertia (deeper beam), use a stiffer material (steel vs wood), add supports, or reduce the span.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is moment of inertia?

Moment of inertia (I) measures a beam resistance to bending. It depends on the cross-section shape. Deeper beams have much higher I than wider beams of the same area.

What is the modulus of elasticity?

E (modulus of elasticity) measures material stiffness. Steel is about 29,000,000 psi. Wood is about 1,500,000–1,800,000 psi depending on grade.

What is L/360?

L/360 is a common deflection limit. It means the maximum deflection should not exceed the span length divided by 360. For a 10 ft span, that is 0.33 inches.

Can I use this for steel beams?

Yes, but ensure you use the correct moment of inertia for the steel section. This calculator uses E = 29,000,000 psi, which is standard for steel.
