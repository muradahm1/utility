The Small Business & Freelancer Playbook

The essential unit economics and tax rules every founder and freelancer must know.

Rule 1

Gross Margin vs. Net Profit: Revenue is Vanity, Cash is Reality

A business can make $1,000,000 in sales and still go bankrupt if margins are thin. Gross margin measures how much money remains after directly delivering your product or service. Net profit is what is actually left after paying software, rent, marketing, and taxes.

Rule 2

Calculate Your Break-Even Point Before Spending Money

Your break-even sales volume tells you the exact number of units, subscriptions, or consulting hours you must sell each month just to pay the bills and hit zero. Every dollar sold past that number is pure pre-tax profit.

Rule 3

The Freelancer Golden Rule: Save 25% to 30% Immediately

Unlike W-2 workers, 1099 contractors and freelancers do not have taxes withheld automatically. Transfer 25% to 30% of every invoice into a high-yield business savings account the day the client pays to comfortably cover quarterly IRS 1040-ES payments.

Plain-English Terms You Need to Know

Gross Margin
The percentage of revenue left over after subtracting direct costs: (Revenue - COGS) ÷ Revenue × 100.
Break-Even Point
The point where total business revenue equals total fixed and variable costs, meaning zero loss and zero profit.
LTV (Customer Lifetime Value)
The total estimated gross profit a single customer brings to your business across the entire duration of your relationship.
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
Total marketing and sales dollars spent divided by the number of new paying customers gained.
